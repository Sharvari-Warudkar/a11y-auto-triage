# WCAG 2.1 Success Criterion 2.4.6: Headings and Labels (Level AA)

## Requirement
Headings and labels describe topic or purpose.

## Who is affected
- Users with cognitive disabilities who rely on clear headings to understand page structure
- Screen reader users who navigate by headings
- Users with attention difficulties who scan pages by headings
- Users with low vision who enlarge text and rely on headings to orient

## Common failures
- Generic headings ("Section 1", "Content", "More Info")
- Form labels that don't describe what to enter ("Field 1", "Input")
- Headings that don't match the content they introduce
- Missing headings where visual sections exist
- Duplicate headings on the same page with different content

## Sufficient techniques
- G130: Providing descriptive headings
- G131: Providing descriptive labels

## Remediation
1. Write headings that clearly describe the section content
2. Use labels that tell users exactly what information is expected
3. Ensure heading hierarchy reflects document structure (h1 > h2 > h3)
4. Don't skip heading levels (h1 → h3 without h2)
5. Each form field should have a label that describes the expected input
